# Reward-free World Models for Online Imitation Learning (IQ-MPC)

- **Authors:** Shangzhe Li, Zhiao Huang, **Hao Su**
- **Group role:** senior/last author (Hao Su; affiliated with UCSD and Hillbot in the paper)
- **Date published:** 2025-07 (ICML 2025, Vancouver; PMLR vol. 267). arXiv v1 2024-10-17; v5 2025-05-11.

## Summary
IQ-MPC is a TD-MPC-style, decoder-free latent world model for online imitation learning with no reward signal. It has no reward head; instead the critic is trained with an inverse soft-Q (IQ-Learn) objective on expert and behaviour buffers. At planning time, rewards are decoded from the critic as r = Q(z,a) − γV(z′), and MPPI plans in latent space using a policy prior.

## 3. Datasets
Expert demonstrations are generated by trained TD-MPC2 agents. They are not released as far as found.
- **DMControl locomotion:** 100 trajectories (Hopper, Walker, Quadruped, Cheetah), 500 for Humanoid, 1000 for Dog; 500 steps each.
- **MyoSuite dexterous manipulation:** 100 trajectories × 100 steps per task (Key Turn, Object Hold, Pen Twirl).
- **ManiSkill2:** appendix E.2.
- **Visual DMControl:** 100 RGB expert trajectories per task.

The environments themselves are public.

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
Metrics: episode reward and success rate; 3 seeds. Baselines: online IQ-Learn+SAC, CFIL+SAC, HyPE.
- **Locomotion (Fig. 2):** near-expert and more stable than the baselines. Comparable to HyPE on Quadruped Run and Cheetah Run; better elsewhere. Dog results are in App. E.1.
- **MyoSuite success rate (Table 1):**

  | Task | IQ-MPC | IQL+SAC | CFIL+SAC | HyPE |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Key Turn | 0.87±0.03 | 0.72 | 0.65 | 0.55 |
  | Object Hold | 0.96±0.03 | 0.00 | 0.01 | 0.13 |
  | Pen Twirl | 0.73±0.05 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

- **Visual DMControl (Fig. 4):** beats IQL+SAC with a conv encoder on Cheetah Run and Walker Run; comparable on Walker Walk.
- **Expert-count ablation (Fig. 5):** expert-level with 10 demos on Hopper Hop and 5 on Object Hold; unstable with 5 on Hopper Hop.
- **Reward recovery (App. G):** decoded rewards correlate positively with the ground-truth reward (inverse RL use).

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- Components: encoder h, latent dynamics d, Q-function, policy prior π (TD-MPC2-style MLPs); a shallow conv encoder for pixels. No reward model.
- Loss: latent consistency with stop-gradient (Eq. 11), plus an inverse soft-Q critic loss with χ² regularization (Eq. 12), plus a Wasserstein-1 gradient penalty for critic/policy balance (Eq. 14). Max-entropy policy loss with fixed β (Eq. 13).
- Planner: MPPI with decoded soft rewards and a terminal V (Algorithm 1, Eq. 15).
- Theory: the objectives minimize a suboptimality bound (Lemma 4.1, from Kolev et al. 2024).

## 6. Conclusions and future work
- **Authors:**
  - The method gives stable, expert-level online imitation on high-dimensional locomotion and dexterous manipulation, including from pixels.
  - Limitations (App. I): sensitive to observation/action dimensionality, because the discriminator can become too strong in low-dimensional settings.
  - Future work: more robust stabilization; real-robot experiments.
- **Own observations:**
  - The experts are TD-MPC2 agents in simulation, which likely makes imitation easier than with human demos.
  - Only 3 seeds.
  - No code or weights, which hurts reproducibility.
