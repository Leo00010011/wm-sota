# Fighting Uncertainty with Gradients: Offline Reinforcement Learning via Diffusion Score Matching

- **Authors:** H. J. Terry Suh, Glen Chou, Hongkai Dai, Lujie Yang, Abhishek Gupta, **Russ Tedrake**
- **Group role:** Senior author (Tedrake last author). First author and co-first authors are Tedrake-lab members (MIT CSAIL / TRI).
- **Date published:** 2023-11 (CoRL 2023, Atlanta, 6 to 9 November 2023; PMLR vol. 229); arXiv v1 2023-06-24
- **Venue:** 7th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2023), PMLR 229:2878–2904
- **Links:** arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14079 · PMLR https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/suh23a.html · Project https://sites.google.com/view/score-guided-planning/home · Code https://github.com/hjsuh94/score_po
- **Number of citations:** 18 (source: Semantic Scholar API, checked 2026-10-13)

## Summary
The paper is about offline model-based RL with a learned dynamics (world) model. To stop the planner from exploiting model errors, it penalises a smoothed distance to the training data. The gradient of that distance equals the score of a noise-perturbed data distribution, so it can be learned directly with diffusion-style denoising score matching. Score-Guided Planning (SGP) then does first-order (gradient) trajectory optimisation through the learned dynamics model, with a score-based data-likelihood term. This scales to pixel-space action spaces where ensembles and zeroth-order planners fail.

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
- **Metrics:** D4RL normalised return (5 trials), task cost on cart-pole and pixel tasks, qualitative rollout fidelity on hardware.
- **D4RL (Table 1)**, SGP vs the MOPO ensemble baseline:

  | Task | SGP | MOPO |
  |---|---|---|
  | random halfcheetah / hopper / walker2d | 31.3 / 12.3 / 15.6 | 35.4 / 11.7 / 13.6 |
  | medium halfcheetah / hopper / walker2d | 46.6 / 60.6 / 36.2 | 42.3 / 28.0 / 17.8 |
  | medium-expert halfcheetah / hopper / walker2d | 61.6 / 109.2 / 103.0 | 63.3 / 23.7 / 44.6 |

  - Diffuser is stronger on medium tasks (e.g. walker2d-medium 79.6). CQL and BC are strong on some medium-expert tasks.
  - The authors claim performance "comparable" to the state of the art, and better than MOPO on many tasks with less memory.
- **Cart-pole swing-up with learned dynamics (Fig. 2):** SGP plans are followed closely under true dynamics. Adam plus ensembles gets stuck in local minima; CEM is slower or worse.
- **Pixel single integrator (Fig. 3, App. C):** a pixel action space with up to 15,360 decision variables. SGP beats vanilla MBRL, Adam plus ensembles and CEM plus ensembles.
- **Hardware box pushing (Fig. 4):** SGP keeps predicted marker rollouts on the implicit rigid-body constraint manifold and reaches different goals set by reward from one demonstration dataset. Vanilla MBRL (β=0) breaks the keypoint structure.
- **Downstream tasks:** offline MBRL planning and control (MPC), imitation with reward re-targeting.

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- **World model:** a learned dynamics f_θ(x, u) trained by regression on offline transitions. The cart-pole model is an MLP (64, 64, 32). Ensembles of 6 MLPs are used as the baseline.
- **Uncertainty:** smoothed distance to data d_σ², equivalent to −σ² log p_σ (Gaussian-perturbed empirical distribution). Its gradient (the score) is learned with denoising / annealed score matching over a noise schedule σ_k. This is "diffusion" in the sense of score models; no sample generation is involved.
- **Planner (SGP):** single-shooting gradient ascent on Σ r_t + βσ² Σ log p_σ(x_t, u_t), backpropagating through f_θ and adding learned score terms (Eq. 11, Algo. 1). Annealing σ drives iterates onto the data. Used in MPC.
- **Theory:** data stability of score-based penalties, and Lipschitz-based bounds on model bias, vs ensembles that can have spurious minima.

## 6. Conclusions and future work
- **Authors:**
  - SGP beats zeroth-order methods and ensembles, is comparable to state-of-the-art offline RL, and scales to 15,360 decision variables.
  - Limitations: it discourages extrapolation by construction; MPC takes 1 to 2 s per iteration, which is a bottleneck for real time; aleatoric uncertainty is not investigated.
- **Own observations:**
  - The world model itself is simple (state-based MLP). The contribution is the uncertainty-aware planner, not the model.
  - D4RL results are mixed (SGP is clearly below Diffuser and CQL on several tasks).
  - Hardware evaluation is qualitative.
