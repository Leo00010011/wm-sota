## Summary
PropNet is a learned, differentiable, graph-based physics simulator (a structured world model). It extends Interaction Networks with multi-step effect propagation within one time step, so that forces travel instantly through chains of objects (Newton's cradle, ropes). For partially observable scenes it learns a latent dynamics model over an encoded scene. The learned model is used for model-predictive control (gradient-based shooting) and for online estimation of physical attributes.

- All synthetic and generated by the authors' simulators (regenerated by the training scripts; no fixed public download needed):
  - Newton's cradle: 2,000 rollouts × 1,000 steps (85/15 split).
  - Rope: 5,000 rollouts × 100 steps, trained on 15-particle ropes, tested on lengths 10 to 20.
  - Box pushing: top-down partial observation, only red boxes visible.

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
- **Forward simulation (MSE):** Newton's cradle over 1,000 steps: PropNet 7.85 vs Interaction Networks 336.46 (Sec. V-A). On rope, PropNet tracks ground truth much better than IN (Fig. 6). Ablation over propagation steps L shows L=3 is the best accuracy/speed trade-off.
- **Open-loop control (Newton's cradle):** optimise the initial angle; angle MSE PropNet 3.08 vs IN 296.66 (Sec. V-B).
- **Rope manipulation (MPC):** beats PD control (MSE 2.50), model-free PPO and IN under known attributes ("Normal"). With 15% attribute noise ("Bias"), online attribute adaptation via SGD recovers the performance ("Adapt"). Generalises to rope lengths 10 to 20 (PD MSE 2.72). Exact PropNet bar values are given only in Fig. 7a.
- **Box pushing under partial observation (MPC in latent space):** lower Chamfer distance than PPO and IN (Fig. 7b; values only in the bar chart).
- **Downstream tasks:** model-based control (open-loop and MPC), online system identification, generalisation to new object counts.

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- **Model:** a graph neural network over objects (vertices) and relations (directed edges).
  - Relation and object encoders are MLPs (3×150-d and 1×100-d hidden). Light propagators (1×100-d hidden) are applied L times per time step, with residual connections and shared encodings (Eq. 5–7).
  - Output: next-step velocities (fully observable) or a 100-d latent (partially observable).
- **Partial observation:** encoder τ = parameter-free aggregation ω of PropNet object encodings g. The latent dynamics φ uses a short history window. A PropNet decoder ψ adds an auto-encoding loss to avoid collapse (Eq. 8).
- **Objectives:** L2 forward-prediction loss, plus reconstruction loss for the latent case.
- **Action conditioning:** controls are part of the graph (initial velocities, external forces, pusher state).
- **Planner:** gradient-based shooting via backprop through the model, in an MPC loop (Alg. 1). Attributes such as mass and friction are estimated online by gradient descent.

## 6. Conclusions and future work
- **Authors:** PropNet is a general learnable physics engine that outperforms prior state of the art by a large margin and supports model-based control in fully and partially observable settings. No explicit future-work section.
- **Own observations:**
  - All experiments are 2D or simple simulated scenes. The model needs object-level state or graph structure (no raw pixels except via red-box positions), and there are no real-robot experiments.
  - Several key control results are reported only as bar charts.
  - The code depends on old library versions.
