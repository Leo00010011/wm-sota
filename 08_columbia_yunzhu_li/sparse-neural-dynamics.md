## Summary
The paper learns ReLU neural dynamics models (MLPs or GNNs over keypoints and object states). It then progressively sparsifies them by replacing ReLUs with identity or zero, using a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of a mixed-integer architecture search. With few ReLUs left, model-predictive control over the learned world model is solved as a mixed-integer program with branch-and-bound (Gurobi), which can reach global optimality. This gives better closed-loop control than sampling-based planning (MPPI) or model-free RL, despite a small loss of prediction accuracy.

## 3. Datasets
All datasets are simulated and generated by the authors (Sec. 4):
- **Piecewise affine functions:** 1,600 transition pairs.
- **Object pushing:** Pymunk, 50,000 pairs; the observation is 4 keypoints.
- **Object sorting:** Pymunk, 150,000 pairs with two objects. A GNN is used and tested with up to 8 objects.
- **Rope manipulation:** NVIDIA FleX, 60,000 pairs; the observation is 4 keypoints.
- **Gym:** CartPole-v1 and Reacher-v4.

No public dataset release was found.

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
- **System recovery:** a model with 576 ReLUs is sparsified in 7 rounds (ε from 25 down to 2). The 2-ReLU model recovers the region partition of two 4-piece piecewise affine functions with near-zero error (Sec. 4.1, Fig. 2).
- **Long-horizon prediction (Fig. 3a):** sparsified models stay close to the full model, with overlapping 25th to 75th percentile bands over 100 trials, for pushing, sorting (2 and 3 piles) and rope.
- **Open-loop trajectory optimisation (Fig. 3b):** MIP (Gurobi) "consistently outperforms" MPPI at every sparsity level.
- **Closed-loop MPC (Fig. 5):**
  - There is a sweet spot in ReLU count where closed-loop cost is best.
  - The method beats PPO and SAC with the same interaction budget on pushing, sorting, rope (no PPO on rope), CartPole and Reacher.
  - Values are given only in plots.
- **Real world (Fig. 4):** a robot performs object pushing, rope shaping and sorting of up to 8 objects (qualitative).
- **Downstream tasks:** model-based planning and closed-loop control (pushing, sorting, rope shaping, CartPole, Reacher).

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- **World model:** an autoregressive ReLU MLP (pushing, rope, affine) or GNN (sorting). It predicts the next observation from a short history of observations and actions and is trained with a multi-step simulation loss (Eq. 2).
- **Sparsification:**
  - Each neuron gets a categorical choice {ReLU, identity, zero}, relaxed with Gumbel-Softmax and a straight-through estimator.
  - A regulariser penalises the ReLU probability.
  - The ReLU budget ε is lowered iteratively, freezing the top-ranked neurons to identity or zero (Sec. 3.3–3.4).
- **Planner:** big-M mixed-integer linear encoding of the remaining ReLUs, with bounds from the dataset (Eq. 11). MPC is solved with Gurobi; MPPI is the baseline.
- **Size:** the full piecewise-affine model has 4 hidden layers and 576 ReLUs. Other parameter counts not stated.

## 6. Conclusions and future work
- **Authors:** sparsifying neural dynamics enables efficient branch-and-bound planning and better closed-loop control across MLPs and GNNs. They hope it spurs work that merges learning and control.
- **Authors' limitations:**
  - MIP has worst-case exponential runtime, so much larger models may be intractable.
  - Performance will suffer if heavy sparsification makes predictions useless.
- **My own observations:**
  - The observation spaces are low-dimensional (4 keypoints or object positions), with no perception from pixels.
  - Closed-loop gains are shown only as plots.
  - Gurobi is a commercial solver.
  - No public repository makes reproduction harder.
