# DINO-WM: World Models on Pre-trained Visual Features enable Zero-shot Planning

- **Authors:** Gaoyue Zhou, Hengkai Pan, **Yann LeCun**, Lerrel Pinto
- **Group role:** Co-author (LeCun, third author). Senior/last author is Lerrel Pinto (NYU Courant), who is not a PI of another group in the summary, so the paper stays in this folder. Corresponding author: Gaoyue Zhou (NYU).

- **Venue:** ICML 2025 (42nd International Conference on Machine Learning), poster.
- **Links:** arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04983 · Proceedings https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/zhou25t.html · ICML poster page https://icml.cc/virtual/2025/poster/46026 · Project page https://dino-wm.github.io · Code https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm
- **Number of citations:** 472 (source: Semantic Scholar API, checked 2026-10-13)

## Summary
DINO-WM is an action-conditioned latent world model that predicts future DINOv2 patch features with a causal ViT predictor, without reconstructing pixels. Trained on offline, reward-free trajectories, it is used for zero-shot goal-image reaching by planning (MPC with CEM) to minimize the latent distance between the predicted final state and the goal embedding.

## 3. Datasets
All are simulated and generated by the authors; released on OSF (same link as above). Sizes are from Appendix A.1:
- PointMaze (D4RL): 2,000 random trajectories.
- Wall (custom 2-room navigation): 1,920 trajectories × 50 steps; 10,240 trajectories for the randomized-wall variant.
- Reacher (DMC): 3,000 trajectories × 100 steps.
- Push-T: 18,500 samples (noisy replays of expert trajectories); 20,000 trajectories × 100 steps for the shape/color variant.
- Rope and Granular (NVIDIA FleX, XArm): 1,000 trajectories × 20 steps each.
- Evaluation: 50 random start/goal pairs (Maze, Wall, Reach, PushT) and 10 instances (Rope, Granular).

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
- Metrics: planning success rate (SR), Chamfer distance (CD) for deformables, LPIPS/SSIM of decoded predictions.
- Baselines: IRIS, DreamerV3, TD-MPC2 (all retrained offline without reward, used with MPC), AVDC (diffusion video model); encoder ablations R3M, ResNet-18, DINO CLS.
- Goal-conditioned planning (Table 1), DINO-WM vs. best baseline: Maze SR 0.98 (DreamerV3 1.00), Wall 0.96 (DreamerV3 1.00), Reach 0.92 (DreamerV3 0.64), PushT 0.90 (IRIS 0.32), Rope CD 0.41 (IRIS 1.11), Granular CD 0.26 (IRIS 0.37). TD-MPC2 gets 0.00 without reward signal.
- Encoder ablation (Table 2): DINOv2 patch features beat R3M, ResNet and DINO CLS, e.g. PushT 0.90 vs 0.44 (DINO CLS).
- Generalization to unseen configurations (Table 3): WallRandom SR 0.82 (DreamerV3 0.76), PushObj 0.34 (best baseline 0.18), GranularRandom CD 0.63 (IRIS 0.86).
- Prediction quality (Table 4, LPIPS): PushT 0.007 vs 0.039 (DINO CLS), Granular 0.035 vs 0.080.
- Appendix A.4.1: planning success and prediction quality improve with dataset size (200 to 18,500 trajectories on PushT). CEM outperforms gradient-descent planning (App. A.5.3).

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- Encoder: frozen DINOv2 ViT-S/14, patch features 14×14×384 on 196×196 inputs (App. A.9).
- Predictor: ViT without tokenizer (decoder-only transformer), depth 6, 16 heads, MLP dim 2048, about 19M parameters; frame-level causal attention mask; history length H.
- Action conditioning: actions (and proprioception) are mapped by an MLP and concatenated to every patch token.
- Objective: teacher-forced latent MSE between predicted and encoded next-frame patch features (Eq. 1). Optional transposed-conv decoder trained separately, for visualization only; adding its loss to the predictor hurt planning (App. A.4.3).
- Planner: MPC with CEM on a terminal latent MSE cost to the goal embedding; gradient descent also tested.

## 6. Conclusions and future work
- Authors: latent-space world modeling on pretrained patch features captures dynamics, generalizes to unseen configurations and allows zero-shot planning without rewards, demos or inverse models. Stated limitations and future work: needs offline data with good state-action coverage (could combine with exploration); needs ground-truth actions, so it cannot yet use action-free internet video; a hierarchical planner combining high-level planning and low-level control is a proposed extension.
- Own observations: all tasks are simulated with short horizons (e.g. PushT goals feasible within 25 steps); no real-robot results. CEM planning over a 19M-parameter predictor on 196 patch tokens is slow per decision (App. A.8 reports times), which LeWM later targets. Baselines were run offline without rewards, a setting they were not designed for, which partly explains TD-MPC2's 0.00.
