# Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models

- **Authors:** Vlad Sobal, Wancong Zhang, Kyunghyun Cho, **Randall Balestriero**, Tim G. J. Rudner, **Yann LeCun**
- **Group role:** Senior (last) author: LeCun; Balestriero co-author. Affiliations: NYU, Meta FAIR, Brown.
- **Date published:** 2025-12 (NeurIPS 2025 conference, December 2025). arXiv v1: 2025-02-20; v4: 2025-10-29.
- **Venue:** NeurIPS 2025, main conference, poster (OpenReview venue "NeurIPS 2025 poster"). An earlier version was a poster at the ICLR 2025 World Models workshop ("Stress-Testing Offline Reward-Free RL...").
- **Links:** arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14819 · NeurIPS poster page https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/116649 · Project page https://latent-planning.github.io · Code https://github.com/vladisai/PLDM

## Summary
The paper compares reward-free offline RL (GCIQL, HIQL, HILP, CRL, GCBC) with a model-based method, PLDM (Planning with a Latent Dynamics Model): a JEPA-style latent dynamics model trained without reconstruction and used for goal-reaching with MPPI planning. On navigation tasks with controlled data quality, PLDM is the most robust overall and the only method that generalizes to unseen maze layouts.

## 3. Datasets
All generated by the authors (23 datasets in total); download instructions in `pldm_envs/` READMEs.
- Two-Rooms (point mass, 64×64 two-channel top-down images): default 3M transitions (about 30k trajectories of length 91); variants with episode lengths 91/64/32/16, random-policy data, no-door-passing data, and smaller sizes down to a few thousand transitions (Sections 4.1–4.5).
- Diverse PointMaze (MuJoCo PointMaze, 64×64 RGB, random 4×4 layouts): 1M transitions each from 5, 10, 20 or 40 layouts, episodes of 100 steps (Appendix C.2, Table 3).
- Ant-U-Maze (OGBench-style, 29-D state, 8-D action): 5M transitions from a noisy directional expert, trajectory lengths 25–500 (Section 4.7).

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
- Metric: goal-reaching success rate (3 seeds); chase-avoidance success (keep distance ≥ 1.4 over 100 steps).
- Two-Rooms, good-quality data (Table 2): PLDM 97.8 ± 0.7, HILP 100.0, GCIQL 98.0, HIQL 96.4, CRL 89.3, GCBC 86.0.
- No door-passing trajectories (stitching, Table 2): HILP 100.0, GCIQL 99.6, PLDM 34.4, HIQL 26.3, CRL 14.7, GCBC 8.4.
- Data efficiency (Fig. 4): PLDM and GCIQL keep about 80% success with a few thousand transitions. Random-policy data: PLDM, HILP and GCIQL are most robust.
- New task (chase avoidance, Fig. 5): PLDM, by flipping the cost sign, beats HILP.
- Ant-U-Maze stitching (Fig. 6): PLDM, HIQL and HILP reach 100% with short trajectories; others fail.
- Unseen maze layouts (Fig. 2, 8): PLDM generalizes best, even from 5 training layouts; all methods reach 97–100% on a single fixed layout (Table 4: PLDM 0.990, GCIQL/HIQL/HILP 1.000).
- Summary table (Table 1): PLDM is the only method rated at least "average" on all six properties.
- Appendix G: reconstruction-based features (DreamerV3) underperform for test-time planning.

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- Encoder h_θ (from images) and K action-conditioned predictors f_θ^k (ensemble for uncertainty), trained JEPA-style: multi-step latent prediction loss (Eq. 3) with no reconstruction.
- Anti-collapse: VICReg-style variance (applied over time) and covariance terms, an inverse-dynamics loss (MLP predicts a_t from z_t, z_{t+1}) and an optional temporal-smoothness term (Appendix D.1.1).
- Planning: MPPI in latent space with cost = latent distance to goal summed over horizon + β × ensemble-variance penalty (Eqs. 5–7); replans every step (about 4× slower than model-free baselines).
- Parameter count: not stated in the main text (not found).

## 6. Conclusions and future work
- Authors: PLDM is robust to data quality, data-efficient, generalizes best to new layouts and to non-goal tasks; HILP-style structured latent spaces stitch well but need more data; model-free GCRL is best with plentiful high-quality data. Future work: manipulation and partially observable domains; better dynamics learning against compounding errors over long horizons; faster test-time planning (gradient-based or amortized).
- Own observations: all environments are low-resolution (64×64) navigation tasks, so the conclusions may not transfer to visually complex or contact-rich settings (the authors acknowledge excluding manipulation). PLDM's stitching weakness (34% with no door-passing data) shows that a one-step dynamics model with myopic latent-distance cost struggles with long-horizon goals, the open problem later addressed by hierarchical planning work from the same group.
