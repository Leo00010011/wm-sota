## Summary
PhysDreamer gives a static 3D Gaussian object an action-conditioned dynamics model. It works in three steps:
1. An image-to-video model (Stable Video Diffusion) generates a reference video of the object moving.
2. A spatially varying Young's-modulus field and an initial velocity field are optimized so that a differentiable MPM simulation, rendered with Gaussian splatting, matches that video.
3. The estimated material field is then used to simulate how the object responds to new forces or drags.

The video model's dynamics prior is thus distilled into physical parameters of a simulator-based world model.

## 3. Datasets
- **Own captures:** 8 real static scenes captured multi-view: 5 flowers, an alocasia plant, a telephone cord and a beanie hat.
- **Real interaction videos:** 4 per scene (poke or drag) for all scenes except the red rose, used as references.
- **Availability:** scenes are on Hugging Face (link above).
- **Size:** 0.5–1.5M Gaussians per scene; foreground objects have 50k–300k.
- **Reference videos:** 14 frames generated by Stable Video Diffusion with a low motion bucket.

## 4. Evaluation and downstream tasks
**User study (2AFC, Table 1):** 100 participants × 8 scenes, giving 800 judgements per comparison.

| PhysDreamer preferred over | Motion realism | Visual quality |
|---|---|---|
| PhysGaussian (same initialization, manual materials) | 80.8% | 65.0% |
| DreamGaussian4D | 63.5% | 70.0% |
| Real captured videos | 53.7% | 37.3% |

- In the comparison with DreamGaussian4D, participants also chose PhysDreamer for "motion amount" in 73.6% of judgements.
- For the alocasia scene, 86% of participants judged PhysDreamer more realistic than the real capture. The authors attribute this to MPM producing smoother, slower motion.

**Multi-view ablation (Sec. 5.4):** for the self-occluding alocasia, two reference views were preferred over one: 81.0% for visual quality and 86.0% for motion realism.

**Other results:** FVD and other quantitative video metrics are reported in the supplement, which I did not read.

**Downstream tasks:** interactive 3D dynamics synthesis for VR or content creation. No planning or robotics tasks.

## 5. Architecture and techniques
- **Representation:** 3D Gaussians, with simulation run directly on the Gaussian particles (as in PhysGaussian), plus optional internal filling.
- **Physics:**
  - Differentiable MPM with a fixed-corotated hyperelastic model.
  - 768 sub-steps between video frames, each about 4.34×10⁻⁵ s, on a 64³ grid.
  - Gradient checkpointing during simulation.
- **Parameters:**
  - Spatially varying Young's modulus E(x) and initial velocity v0(x), each a triplane (8³ and 24³) followed by a 3-layer MLP, with total-variation regularization.
  - Poisson's ratio is constant; mass comes from density × volume.
- **Optimization:**
  - Two stages: first velocity, using the first 3 frames; then E.
  - Loss: 0.1·L1 + 0.9·D-SSIM against the generated video.
  - Gradients are truncated to the previous frame.
- **Acceleration:** K-Means "driving particles", 10–50× fewer than the Gaussians. Each Gaussian follows a local rigid transform fitted to its 8 nearest driving particles.
- **Action conditioning:** external forces or drags applied in the simulator, plus initial velocities.

## 6. Conclusions and future work
**Authors' conclusions:** distilling video-model priors into material fields enables realistic interactive elastic dynamics.

**Authors' stated limitations:**
- The object, its background segmentation and its boundary conditions must be specified manually.
- The method is slow: about 1 minute per second of video on a V100.
- It handles only elastic objects without collisions.

**Own observations:**
- The material estimate depends on the quality of the generated reference video. Short 14-frame SVD clips with small motion can bias the stiffness estimate.
- Evaluation relies on human preference; there is no physical ground truth.
- This paper is the precursor of WonderPlay and PerpetualWonder, which extend the idea to multi-material scenes and long horizons.
